### Objective

React to new information that may invalidate part of your Gate 3 strategy.
You must decide whether to:

- KEEP — no structural change is required
- LOCAL REPAIR — a limited adjustment is sufficient
- PARTIAL RESCHEDULING — a relevant part of the plan must be rebuilt
- FULL RESCHEDULING — the current plan is no longer defensiblereconstruct and clean the production problem;

<div class="box mt-5">
<b>Important:</b> Your role information is private at the beginning of the activity.
</div>


---
title: "Stage 1: Access Your Private Role Information"
layout: default
transition: fade-out
background: /images/Designer-02.png
backgroundSize: cover
---

### Individual work — do not discuss yet


- Open the Moodle forum.
- Read:
  - The common event.
  - Your private role information.
  - Your human-only decision responsibility.
- Do not share your private information with your team yet.

<b>You are personally accountable for understanding your card</b>

<div class="box mt-5">
<b>Instructor control:</b> Do not move to Stage 2 until everyone confirms access.
</div>

---
title: "Stage 2: Individual Assessment"
layout: default
transition: fade-out
background: /images/Designer-02.png
backgroundSize: cover
---

### Work silently and independently

Assess the impact of the new information on the Gate 3 plan.

You must identify:
- What remains valid?
- What becomes invalid or weaker?
- What should be preserved or frozen?
- Which assumptions are now questionable?
- What new risk or opportunity appears?
- Does your role require a human authorization?

Then select one preliminary recommendation:

KEEP | LOCAL REPAIR | PARTIAL RESCHEDULING | FULL RESCHEDULING


<div class="box mt-5">
<b>Remember:</b> No team discussion yet.
</div>


---
title: "Stage 3: Individual Pre-Commitment"
layout: default
transition: fade-out
background: /images/Designer-02.png
backgroundSize: cover
---

### Work silently and independently

Before talking to your team, submit your own position.
Your pre-commitment must include:

<b>1. Your recommendation</b>
KEEP / LOCAL REPAIR / PARTIAL RESCHEDULING / FULL RESCHEDULING

<b>2. Evidence</b>
Which facts support your position?

<b>3. Impact</b>
What is affected and how seriously?

<b>4. Preservation decision</b>
What should not be changed?

<b>5. Human decision point</b>
Which action cannot be delegated to AI?

<b>6. Reconsideration trigger</b>
What new evidence would make you change your recommendation?

<b>Once submitted, do not edit your answer.</b>

This last sentence is especially useful pedagogically: it creates a genuine pre-commitment before social influence appears.

### STOP

Do not discuss private information until the instructor confirms:

<b>“Individual pre-commitment is closed. Team discussion is now open.”</b>

Until that moment:
- no screenshots,
- no copying private cards into the team chat,
- no oral disclosure,
- no collaborative AI prompting using other members’ private information.

You can use AI individually, but <b>AI cannot authorize managerial 
exceptions or replace your role accountability.</b>


---
title: "Stage 4: Controlled Information Release"
layout: default
transition: fade-out
background: /images/Designer-02.png
backgroundSize: cover
---

### Team discussion is now open

Each member has 60–90 seconds to report:

1. What changed from my role perspective?
2. What is the most important new fact?
3. What decision does my role recommend?
4. What must remain human-controlled?
5. What should the team reconsider?

Recommended order:

        R01 → R02 → R03 → … → R10 

If a role is vacant, <b>do not invent its private information.</b>

<div class="box mt-5">
<b>This is important for your teams with missing roles:</b> the absence itself becomes part of the uncertainty/governance problem.
</div>

---
title: "Stage 5: Build the Shared Situation Picture"
layout: default
transition: fade-out
background: /images/Designer-02.png
backgroundSize: cover
---

After all private information has been disclosed, the team must classify every relevant statement as:

- CONFIRMED FACT
- PRIVATE ROLE EVIDENCE
- ASSUMPTION
- INFERENCE
- UNRESOLVED UNCERTAINTY

Then answer collectively:
<b>Which parts of our Gate 3 strategy are still defensible?</b>

and:
<b>Which parts must change?</b>

<div class="box mt-5">
<b>Do not silently repair contradictions or missing information.</b>
</div>

---
title: "Stage 6: Decide the Intervention Scope"
layout: default
transition: fade-out
background: /images/Designer-02.png
backgroundSize: cover
---

### Team Decision

The team must agree on one final intervention level:

<b>KEEP</b>
The original strategy remains defensible.

<b>LOCAL REPAIR</b>
A limited adjustment solves the problem without restructuring the plan.

<b>PARTIAL RESCHEDULING</b>
A substantial subset of operations/resources must be reconsidered.

<b>FULL RESCHEDULING</b>
The current strategy is structurally invalid and must be rebuilt.

The team must explicitly explain:

- why this level is sufficient,
- why a smaller intervention is insufficient,
- why a larger intervention is unnecessary.

<div class="box mt-5">
<b>Remember:</b> That last comparison is valuable because it prevents “FULL RESCHEDULING” becoming the easy defensive answer.
</div>


---
title: "Stage 7: Human Authorization Check"
layout: default
transition: fade-out
background: /images/Designer-02.png
backgroundSize: cover
---

### Who has authority to approve each exception?

Examples:
- maintenance displacement, overtime,
- customer commitment changes, outsourcing,
- alternative routing, financial exceptions,
- quality deviations.

AI may:
- analyse, calculate, simulate, compare alternatives, identify conflicts.

AI may not authorize decisions assigned to accountable human roles.

<b>No authorization → no valid exception.</b>


---
title: "Stage 8: Final Team Decision"
layout: default
transition: fade-out
background: /images/Designer-02.png
backgroundSize: cover
---

### Final decision

Your team must submit:

<b>Final intervention</b>

KEEP / LOCAL REPAIR / PARTIAL RESCHEDULING / FULL RESCHEDULING

and briefly document:

- What changed?
- What was preserved?
- What was modified?
- Which evidence drove the decision?
- Which disagreements remained?
- Which human authorizations were required?
- What uncertainty remains?
- What would trigger another rescheduling decision?

---
title: "Final Reflection"
layout: default
transition: fade-out
background: /images/Designer-02.png
backgroundSize: cover
---

### Comparison

Compare:

<b>Your individual pre-commitment</b>

with

<b>The final team decision</b>

Ask yourself:
  
  Did I change my position?

If yes:
    What evidence or argument changed it?

If no:
    Why was my original position still defensible?

And finally:

Did AI support the decision, or did it influence the decision beyond what could be independently justified?
